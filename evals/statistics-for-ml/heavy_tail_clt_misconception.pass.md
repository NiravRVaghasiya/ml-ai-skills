Not with data like this. Sample size alone doesn't rescue you.

- The CLT needs finite variance. It says the sample mean of i.i.d. draws is approximately normal for large n provided the variance is finite. A true Cauchy distribution has no finite variance (not even a finite mean): the average of 10,000 Cauchy draws is exactly as spread out as a single draw, so the mean never settles down and no n makes a t-test valid. Real latencies are bounded by timeouts, so their variance is technically finite, but with a tail this heavy the normal approximation converges very slowly - the mean and its standard error are dominated by the handful of 1000x requests, and the "n of 30 or more" rule of thumb says nothing here.
- Look at the tail first: a histogram on a log scale or a Q-Q plot of log latency, and how much of the total the top 0.1% of requests contribute.
- Decide what you want to compare. For latency that is usually the median and tail percentiles (p95/p99), not the mean.
- Then use a method that matches: a bootstrap CI on the difference in medians or p99 (resample requests, or users if requests are clustered by user), a trimmed mean, or a t-test on log latency (which compares geometric means, a different quantity). Mann-Whitney is a reasonable rank-based check, but it tests whether one arm tends to produce larger latencies, not a difference in means. Don't bootstrap the raw mean either: with infinite-variance tails the bootstrap of the mean breaks for the same reason the CLT does.
- If you really care about mean latency (e.g. for capacity planning), report it, but treat its CI with suspicion and check how much it moves when the top few requests are removed.
