It's a common simplification, but not what He et al. found. Their key observation was degradation: adding layers to a plain network made training error go up, which can't be overfitting. They used batch normalization and checked that gradients were healthy, so they argued vanishing gradients were unlikely to be the cause and called it an optimization problem. ResNets fix it by learning a residual F(x) added to the identity x - an identity mapping is easy to learn. Better gradient flow through the shortcuts is a real benefit too, but it's not the whole explanation.
