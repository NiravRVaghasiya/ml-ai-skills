Not really. He et al. (2015) were explicit that their deeper plain networks used BatchNorm and careful initialization, and they checked that forward and backward signals had healthy magnitudes, so vanishing gradients were not the explanation. What they observed was degradation: the 56-layer plain net had higher training error than the 20-layer one, an optimization difficulty, not overfitting. Residual blocks reformulate each block as learning F(x) and outputting F(x) + x, so representing the identity just means pushing F toward zero, which is easier to optimize. Skip connections do also help gradient flow, but that's a side benefit rather than the core argument.
