No. Adam's weight_decay argument implements L2 regularization: PyTorch adds wd*w to the gradient, and that term is then normalized by the running second-moment estimate, so weights with large gradients get barely any decay. AdamW decouples weight decay from the gradient update (Loshchilov & Hutter) and applies w <- w - lr*wd*w directly. They are only equivalent for SGD. For the reproduction, use AdamW.
