With components of q and k roughly independent, zero-mean and unit-variance, each dot product is a sum of d_k such terms, so its standard deviation grows like sqrt(d_k). Large logits drive the softmax toward a one-hot output, where its derivative is almost zero, so gradients to the queries and keys nearly disappear and learning slows. Dividing by sqrt(d_k) brings the logits back to unit variance independent of head size. Leave it out and large heads train poorly; it's not what makes the weights sum to one - softmax already does that.
