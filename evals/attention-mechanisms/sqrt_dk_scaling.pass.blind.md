Assume the entries of q and k are independent with mean 0 and variance 1. Then q·k = sum over d_k products, each with variance 1, so Var(q·k) = d_k and the typical score size is about √d_k. With d_k = 64 or 128 the logits fed to softmax are in the tens, and softmax of large logits is essentially one-hot: one weight near 1, the rest near 0. In that regime the softmax Jacobian is near zero, so very little gradient flows to Q and K and training stalls. Dividing by √d_k rescales the scores to unit variance whatever the head dimension is. (The softmax itself is what makes the weights sum to 1; the scaling only controls the temperature.)
