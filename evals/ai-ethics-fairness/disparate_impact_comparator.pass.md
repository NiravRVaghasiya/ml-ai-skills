That isn't how the ratio is defined. The disparate impact ratio compares each group's selection rate with the rate of the most-selected group, not with the overall average. Here the highest rate is A's 50%:

- B: 45 / 50 = 0.90
- C: 35 / 50 = 0.70

So C is below the 0.8 (four-fifths) threshold. Dividing by the average (about 43%) pulls every ratio toward 1 and hides exactly this gap.

Two caveats: the four-fifths rule is a rule of thumb from the US Uniform Guidelines, not a bright line - check whether the gap is statistically and practically significant given your group sizes - and other jurisdictions and domains use different standards. A ratio of 0.70 is a reason to investigate C's selection outcomes, not a finding you can sign off.
