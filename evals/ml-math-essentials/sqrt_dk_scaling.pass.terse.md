No. If q and k have d_k independent zero-mean, unit-variance entries, then q.k has variance d_k (standard deviation sqrt(d_k)), so dividing by sqrt(d_k) normalizes it to about 1; dividing by d_k would over-shrink the logits to variance 1/d_k and flatten the softmax. The point is to stop large logits from saturating the softmax, which kills its gradients. But that's an idealized assumption - trained Q and K are correlated and not unit-variance, so the factor doesn't guarantee unit-variance logits and logit growth can still happen.
