You might think you don't need model.eval() because no_grad() looks like an inference switch, but you do. torch.no_grad() only disables gradient tracking: no autograd graph is built, and nothing else changes. model.eval() is what turns Dropout off and makes BatchNorm use its running statistics. Without it, Dropout keeps zeroing activations at random (noisy, lower accuracy), and BatchNorm normalizes with per-batch statistics and keeps updating its running statistics from your validation batches, even under no_grad. Call model.eval() before validation and model.train() before training resumes, together with no_grad or torch.inference_mode.
